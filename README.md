# Fihrist

**Crop-rotation decision support for water-stressed farms, built on NASA data.**
2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge — *Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data*.

**Live app:** https://app.fihrist.earth · **License:** MIT

> Fihrist supports, and does not replace, farmer and agronomist judgment.

---

## What it does

Start on a 3D globe, fly down to your land and tap the corners of your field — or use a map click, a village search, or one of four presets. Fihrist pulls 24 years of daily NASA POWER weather for that point and compares **three crop rotations** — what the farmer does now, a water-saving rotation and a regenerative rotation — on:

- **water** — irrigation withdrawal or unmet crop water, m³/ha
- **food** — people fed and protein per hectare
- **soil nitrogen** — fixed vs removed, and its hidden replacement cost ("true profit")
- **soil protection** — months of bare soil vs living roots
- **risk** — P10 / P50 / P90 across 2,400 simulated futures, and climate-stress sliders (+0 to +3.5 °C, −40 % to +20 % rain)

With an outlined field, results are also given for the whole field (e.g. cubic metres of canal water per year for your 2.4 ha). The outline sets the field's size, not its weather: NASA POWER describes a ~50 km cell.

Each rotation card explains **why it is suggested, the NASA evidence, expected benefits, information gaps, and local risks** for a farmer or agronomist to review. Farmer priority sliders re-rank the rotations. The interface is available in English, Russian and Tajik.

**Principle:** the equations produce the numbers; nothing is generated by AI. Every constant carries a source and an evidence level (*sourced*, *proxy*, or *TODO*), shown in the app's Methodology drawer and in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Why "Fihrist"

*Fihrist* (Persian/Arabic: "index", "catalogue") honours Ibn al-Nadim's 10th-century *Kitab al-Fihrist*, which catalogued knowledge across disciplines; the empirical traditions of Al-Biruni and Ibn Sina are part of the same heritage. Climate, water, soil, nutrition and money are usually analysed in separate tools. Fihrist connects them in one auditable calculation for one specific field.

## How it works

| Step | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Weather | NASA POWER daily API (community AG), 2001–2024: Tmax, Tmin, precipitation, solar radiation, wind, humidity | NASA POWER |
| Reference ET | FAO-56 Penman–Monteith (daily), Hargreaves cross-check | Allen et al. 1998, FAO-56 |
| Crop water use | Kc curve with climate adjustment, ETc = Kc·ET0 | FAO-56 Tables 11–12, Eqs. 62, 65, 66 |
| Soil water | Daily root-zone balance; fallow evaporation; irrigation at the readily-available-water threshold | FAO-56 ch. 7–8, Tables 19, 22 |
| Yield under water stress | 1 − Ya/Ym = Ky (1 − ETa/ETc) | FAO-33 |
| Food | kcal and protein from USDA FoodData Central; people fed = kcal / (2,200 × 365) | USDA FDC |
| Nitrogen | removal = grain protein / 6.25; legume fixation (one source, scaled by yield) | FAO FNP 77; GRDC 2015 |
| Hidden nitrogen cost | N debt / 0.46 × urea price → true profit | World Bank Pink Sheet |
| Stress calendar | hot and frost days; Fihrist Compound Stress Index from up to four z-scores against the field's own 2001–2024 history: heat, dryness, root-zone wetness, cropland NDVI | NASA POWER; MODIS MOD13Q1 |
| Satellite layers (presets) | MODIS Terra NDVI averaged over ESA WorldCover cropland within 5 km; NASA POWER root-zone wetness (GWETROOT); NASADEM slope | Microsoft Planetary Computer |
| Uncertainty | 2,400 twelve-year futures, year bootstrap of real NASA POWER years, common random numbers across rotations | — |

The engine is plain TypeScript, runs in the browser, and is unit-tested (196 tests), including FAO-56 worked examples (e.g. Example 18, ET0 = 3.9 mm/day) and a closed soil-water balance.

## Validation (Kansas)

Rainfed continuous winter wheat simulated at the Kansas preset vs **USDA NASS statewide Kansas wheat yields, 2015–2024** (the model's potential yield comes from irrigated Kansas yields, not from these numbers):

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Year-to-year correlation | **r = 0.77** |
| Same direction as NASS | **8 of 9** years |
| Mean level | **−45 %** (model 1.64 vs NASS 2.99 t/ha) |

The model follows real good and bad years, but its rainfed level is too low. Likely reasons — statewide average vs one dry west-central grid cell, no winter dormancy, and FAO bare-soil evaporation emptying the summer fallow — are shown in the app's Validation drawer. FAO stage-specific water-stress factors were also tested and fitted worse, so they are not used. We report the gap rather than tune it away: rainfed numbers are for comparing rotations, not for forecasting a harvest. The OpenET evapotranspiration comparison is **pending** (needs data access).

*Validated in Kansas where ground truth exists, applied in Tajikistan and Afghanistan where it does not.*

## What only the combination shows

Weather, soil water and satellite greenness usually sit in separate tools. Fihrist puts them on one monthly grid and asks where they **disagree**:

- **Compound stress month:** at least max(2, n − 1) of the n available signals are abnormal (z > 1) at the same time.
- **Unexplained vegetation stress:** crops much less green than usual (NDVI z > 1.5) while heat, rain and soil water were all normal (z < 0.5). The cause lies outside the weather record: a canal cut, failed sowing, pests, land-use change, or something no dataset here measures.
- **Green despite drought:** a dry month (rain or soil-water z > 1) in which crops stayed at least as green as usual. This usually means irrigation buffered the field.

These are flagged as **questions for the farmer**, not conclusions. Thresholds are project choices, listed in SOURCES.md.

**A first result (a hypothesis, not proof).** All three Central Asian presets show unexplained vegetation stress in **March–April 2012**: Gissar and Khatlon in Tajikistan, and Balkh in Afghanistan, up to about 250 km apart. Kansas does not. Weather, rain and soil water look normal for those months, yet crops were far less green: in March, NDVI was 0.26 vs a normal of 0.42 in Gissar, 0.19 vs 0.28 in Khatlon, and 0.17 vs 0.28 in Balkh. The weather record holds a candidate cause that the index does not yet use: the 2011–12 winter was unusually cold at all three sites.

| Winter 2011–12 | Gissar | Khatlon | Balkh |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frost days, February (normal) | 27 (19.1) | 24 (10.9) | 21 (9.6) |
| Frost days, March (normal) | 19 (7.5) | 11 (3.0) | 8 (2.5) |
| Days ≤ −10 °C, February (normal) | 8 (1.6) | 2 (0.3) | 0 (0.2) |

Winter-kill or delayed green-up after a hard winter would fit this pattern. The three sites share one regional winter, so they are not independent tests. The pattern needs confirmation from field reports before it is treated as a finding. A fifth signal, winter cold, is the obvious next step. NASA POWER is a ~50 km grid, and this local frost record has not been cross-checked against station data.

## Honest findings

- In irrigated Gissar, the "water-saving" rotation uses **more** water than continuous wheat (it adds summer crops); against a wheat–cotton baseline it saves water.
- The regenerative rotation feeds the most people per hectare and keeps the soil covered almost all year, but uses roughly three times the irrigation water.
- With full canal supply assumed, weather changes how much water crops take, not the harvest; water cuts are not modelled yet.

## Limitations

- NASA POWER describes a ~50 km cell; a farm is ~1 ha.
- Not yet modelled: runoff and capillary rise, wheat winter dormancy, nitrogen losses, canal allocation limits, farm costs other than nitrogen.
- Several yields and prices are proxies (listed in each card and in SOURCES.md); local prices should replace international ones.
- Satellite layers (NDVI, soil wetness, slope) are precomputed for the four presets. For any other point they are computed on request ("Run satellite analysis") by an Azure Function in North Europe, next to the Planetary Computer storage (about 20 s); without that, only heat and dryness are used. NDVI is a 5 km cropland average, not the outlined field. Some 16-day composites failed to download; the app shows the count used.
- Russian and Tajik text needs native review.

## Architecture and security

Vite + React + TypeScript + Tailwind, MapLibre GL (3D globe), Leaflet, Recharts, i18next. Hosted on Cloudflare Pages; a Pages Function (`/api/simulate`) runs Monte Carlo batches at the edge, with browser Web Workers as fallback. NASA POWER is called directly from the browser; preset data is prefetched (`scripts/prefetch_power.py`).

Security follows an OWASP Top 10 audit ([SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)): strict Content-Security-Policy without `unsafe-*`, host allowlist, input validation on every boundary, size and time limits, same-origin edge function, self-hosted fonts, pinned dependencies, `npm audit` clean.

```bash
npm install
npm test                 # unit tests
npm run build
npx vite preview --port 4174
node scripts/verify-runtime.mjs   # browser checks: headers, CSP, interactions
```

Requires Node 22.18 or newer.

## Beyond Earth

Every permanent settlement beyond Earth will have to farm in a closed loop. Earth's water-stressed farms already live near those limits. The self-sufficiency measures Fihrist reports — calories per cubic metre of water, nitrogen balance, people fed per hectare — are the same kinds of measures used to evaluate bioregenerative life support. Fihrist shares those constraints; it does not design space habitats.

## Roadmap

- **The exact outline:** satellite layers for the outlined polygon itself rather than a 5 km cropland average (the on-demand Azure service already covers any point).
- **Winter cold as a fifth signal**, to test the 2012 hypothesis above.
- **Check against open ground truth:** AmeriFlux flux towers and PhenoCam cameras (greenness and water use), USDA SCAN and Kansas Mesonet soil moisture, USDA Cropland Data Layer rotations, NOAA GHCN station data for Dushanbe.
- **Farmers without internet:** an SMS summary of the next month's risks; phone photos as optional ground truth.
- Saving several fields, canal allocation limits, local prices, Dari interface.
